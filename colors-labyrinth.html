Dynamic Color-Matching FPS

Colors Labyrinth is a dynamic First-Person Shooter and puzzle-platformer hybrid that served as my foundational deep dive into Unreal Engine 5. The core loop requires players to navigate a maze where environmental interaction is entirely driven by a color-matching mechanic.

Technical Implementation

  • Blueprint Architecture: Programmed the entire gameplay loop, character controller, and environmental logic exclusively using UE5 Blueprints.
  • Dynamic State Machine: Implemented an environmental trigger system where stepping on specific colored platforms dynamically alters the weapon's elemental state and projectile behavior.
  • Actor Communication: Built interaction logic gates ensuring doors only trigger and enemy hitboxes only register damage when hit by projectiles matching their designated color tags.

The Challenge & Learning

As my introduction to Unreal Engine, the most significant hurdle was designing the weapon's state management system. Transitioning colors seamlessly and accurately broadcasting that current state to various interactable actors (doors, targets, enemies) across the map required extensive troubleshooting. Overcoming this solidified my understanding of Actor communication, casting, and event-driven architecture, laying a strong foundation for my subsequent C++ engineering work.
